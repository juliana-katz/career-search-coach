# Warm intro scoping framework

## Rule: Scope warm intros BEFORE cold-applying

Cold apps for senior roles are usually weaker than referred apps. Even a junior internal referrer who emails the recruiter saying "she's worth a look" pulls the resume out of the pile.

## Tier framework

For each role at a target company, identify potential warm contacts and rank by intro value:

| Tier | Titles | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 (highest)** | CMO, VP Marketing, Head of Marketing, CPO (if PMM reports to Product), the actual hiring manager | Direct routing or HM-level visibility |
| **Tier 2 (high)** | Director / Sr Director-level peer in target function; VPs in adjacent functions (Product, Sales) | Peer-level referrer; can vouch internally |
| **Tier 3 (medium)** | Sr Manager / Manager in target function; senior staff in adjacent functions | Referral-eligible; may have HM context |
| **Tier 4 (intel only)** | Recent ex-employees from past 2 years (left in good standing); junior ICs in target function | Culture/HM intel; no referral leverage |

## Don't ask:

- **Junior ICs in unrelated functions** — they can't help
- **People who left on bad terms** — they'll vent, won't help
- **LinkedIn-only connections (no real relationship)** — awkward ask
- **Contacts more than ONE LEVEL JUNIOR to the target role** — asking a Sr Manager to refer for a Head-level role is asymmetric and won't carry weight

## Asymmetric ask rule

A Sr Manager at Mercury cannot credibly refer a candidate for a Head of Growth Marketing role (her boss's boss).

**For peer-and-below contacts, ask for INTEL ONLY:**
- "Curious for your take on Mercury's marketing org and culture — any read?"
- NOT "Can you refer me to the Head of Growth Marketing role?"

## Process per role

1. **Search the user's LinkedIn CSV** for connections at the target company
2. **Categorize matches by tier** (using titles)
3. **Ask the user to confirm relationship strength** for each (LinkedIn connect ≠ knowing them)
4. **If no 1st-degree match:** suggest 2nd-degree search via LinkedIn People → check Mutual Connections
5. **Recommend the right ask** based on contact tier and target role level

## When cold-app is the right call

After warm-intro mapping, cold-app is appropriate if:
- No 1st-degree contacts AND no realistic 2nd-degree path
- The role has been posted recently AND the user is a strong fit (the recruiter will see it)
- The user has a strong resume + tailored application

Even with cold app, **always check if a warm post-hoc follow-up is possible** — a contact who emails the recruiter after the cold app lands can still pull it from the pile.

## The 2nd-degree search

When the user has no 1st-degree match at a target company:

1. Have them search LinkedIn Company → People → filter by title (CMO, VP Marketing, Head of X, Director Y)
2. For each promising person, check "Mutual Connections"
3. The mutual connection is the warm path
4. Surface the 5–10 highest-value candidates back to Claude
5. Claude helps draft the intro request to the mutual

## How to ask a mutual for an intro

Template:

```
Hey [Mutual]!

Quick ask — are you close with [Target Person] at [Company]? I'm exploring my next move
and the [Role] role they have open is a strong fit. If you're comfortable, would you be
open to a quick intro?

Happy to send a forwardable note. Either way, hope you're well!

Cheers,
[User]
```

## Post-coffee chat follow-up

After the user has a meeting with a warm contact, help them:
1. Send a thank-you note within 24 hours
2. Reference one specific thing from the conversation
3. Ask if there's anyone else they'd recommend talking to
4. Update tracker with what was learned
