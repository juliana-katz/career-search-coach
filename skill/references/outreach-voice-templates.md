# Outreach voice templates

## The default template

The right structure for a warm-network outreach (LinkedIn DM, email, etc.) is short — 4 to 5 sentences, ≤ 80 words.

```
Hi [Specific Name]!

[State the connection — Venn cohort, ex-colleague, shared mutual, etc.]

[State what you want — be specific: intel, intro, referral.]

[One-line credential summary — current role + 1–2 prior credentials.]

[Soft close — "I'd love to learn more about [the role/team/company] and how I can contribute."]

Cheers,
[Your name]
```

## Examples

### Cold connect to a recruiter (LinkedIn DM, ≤ 200 chars)

```
Hey [Name]! Not sure if you're the right Talent Partner, but wanted to follow up with a hello.
I'd love to connect and hear more about the [Role] role. I'm currently [current role] with
experience in [credentials].
```

### Warm reach-out to a past colleague (Email, ≤ 100 words)

```
Hi [Name]!

We met ages ago when [shared context — past cohort, prior team, mutual project].

I've followed your career with real interest. There's an open [Role] role at [Company]
I'd love to learn more about — would you have a few minutes to connect?

A quick where-I-am: leading [current role] at [Company], coming from [Prior Role 1] and
[Prior Role 2].

[Optional: mutual friend's name] says hi. Hope to connect soon.

Cheers,
[Your name]
```

### Cold outreach to a senior leader at a target company (LinkedIn DM)

```
Hi [Name] — we have people in common from the [shared community / industry]. I'm currently
[role + 1–2 credential lines]. Considering my next move and would love 15 min to hear what
you're building at [Company].
```

## What NOT to do — common AI / corporate tells to strip

These phrases sound AI-generated or corporate-formal. Strip them:

- ❌ "Quick context on me…"
- ❌ "Following up on my application…"
- ❌ "I figured a direct ping couldn't hurt…"
- ❌ "Exploring my next move…" (coded; just say "considering my next move" or "thinking about what's next")
- ❌ "Would love the chance to connect and hear more…"
- ❌ "Happy to send over my resume or hop on a quick call — whatever's easiest"
- ❌ "feels right up [Company]'s alley"
- ❌ "the combination of [X] maps tightly to what you need"
- ❌ Bio-references ("I noticed you went from X to Y" — reads as stalker-ish)
- ❌ Em-dashes used as connective tissue padding

## Voice rules to enforce

1. **Use contractions.** "I'm," "you're," "I've," "won't" — not "I am," "you are."
2. **Match the user's actual sample.** If they share a message they've sent, copy that structure and length.
3. **Don't pad.** If their template is 35 words, the new draft is 35 words. Don't write 130 words filling in a 35-word template.
4. **Name the recipient.** "Hi Dena!" — not "Hi Talent Team."
5. **Soft contribution close, not transactional ask.** "I'd love to learn more and how I can contribute" beats "Please consider my application."
6. **No formal email throat-clearing.** Open with the person's name and get to the point.
7. **Sign off casually.** "Cheers, Juliana" beats "Best regards, Juliana Katz."

## Eliciting the user's voice

Before writing any draft, ask:
- "Can you share a message you've sent to a peer recently? I want to write in your voice, not mine."
- If they share something — use that exact tone, vocabulary, sentence length.
- If they don't have an example — use the default template above, then let them red-pen it. Iterate until they say "that sounds like me."

## When the user pushes back

If the user says a draft is "too formal," "too corporate," "doesn't sound like me," or "too long":
- **Don't get defensive.** They're calibrating.
- **Cut the AI tells listed above.**
- **Shorten significantly** (often 50% shorter than your first draft).
- **Use their exact template literally** if they give one.
- **Save the lesson** so you don't repeat the same mistake.
